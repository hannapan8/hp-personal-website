LumiViTA

A caregiver–patient coordination platform that helps patients get seen faster and helps caregivers manage their backlog, with Lumi, an AI chat assistant, supporting patients while they wait.

The problem

Waiting is the hardest part of care.

In the ER or urgent care, patients can wait a long time to be seen, and caregivers can be overwhelmed managing the backlog.

Patients

Wait without knowing where they stand or getting any help in the meantime.

Caregivers

Have to decide who to see next across a growing queue.

The idea came from our own teaching. The four of us were TAs for UW's intro Java course, where one assignment had students write a patient prioritization program. Building it showed us how nuanced assigning priority really is, and we wanted to design something that handled it better.

Design goals

Calm for patients, clear for caregivers.

1Feel calm and humanHealthcare is stressful, so the brand should feel warm, not clinical.
2Support patients while they waitGive them a quick way to ask non-emergency questions.
3Help caregivers prioritizeSurface who needs attention most, without anyone getting stuck at the bottom.
4Make it easy to startA clear entry point for both patients and caregivers.
Key design decisions

Four calls that shaped the product.

Decision 01 · Brand

A name and brand built around light

The name went through several rounds in Figma: from a placeholder, to ClearCARE, to Soara, to LumiViTA, combining lumi (light) and vita (life). The tagline evolved with it, from a functional "Streamlining Patient Care with Personalized Insights and Seamless Coordination" to the simpler "Bringing Light Into Your Life." I paired deep navy with bright blues and soft, flowing backgrounds so the brand feels calm and hopeful instead of clinical.

Placeholder→ClearCARE→Soara→LumiViTA
Decision 02 · Support

Lumi, a friendly guide while you wait

Waiting is the most stressful part of getting care, so I designed Lumi, a small character that lives in the bottom-right corner of every screen. Patients can open it to ask quick, non-emergency questions while they wait to be seen. I kept the conversation format familiar, a simple back-and-forth like texting a person, so there's nothing new to learn when you're already anxious. Lumi is powered by GPT-4o.

Decision 03 · Entry

One entry point, personalized by role

Patients and caregivers need very different things, so after signing in, each person picks a role once. That single choice sets up the experience that fits them, without making anyone navigate the other side's tools.

Decision 04 · Triage

Prioritizing by need and by time waited

The triage concept came from real-world triage and our past course project, refined with AI-assisted brainstorming. Each patient gets a priority score from two factors: how severe their symptoms are (mild, moderate, or severe), and how long they've been waiting. Severity weighs most, but wait time keeps rising, so a patient with mild symptoms doesn't get stuck at the bottom of the queue forever. Caregivers see their queue sorted by that score.

How we built it

Designed in Figma, built in 36 hours.

I designed the brand and core screens in Figma, and my three teammates implemented them in code over the 36 hours.

  1. Figma prototypes: the landing page (hero, about, services, team), the role selection screen, the get-started screen, and Lumi.
  2. Hands-on frontend: my laptop couldn't connect to our codebase during the event, so I worked from a teammate's computer on animations and on matching the build to the prototypes.
  3. Logged-in dashboards: the patient and caregiver dashboards were generated with vly.ai, an AI full-stack builder that sponsored the hackathon. I didn't design those screens, so they aren't shown here.

After the hackathon, the backend services were retired, so only the landing page is still live.

Reflection

What I'd change.

We placed Top 6 of 40+ teams in 36 hours, but looking back, I'd push the design further in three places.

Clearer chat bubblesLumi's messages and the patient's messages looked too similar. I'd make them easier to tell apart with color, alignment, and Lumi's avatar beside its replies.
More interactivityThe landing page is mostly static. I'd add motion and interactive moments that show how the product works, not just describe it.
More upfront researchWe designed from our own experience. Next time I'd talk to patients and caregivers before designing, especially about what they'd want from a chatbot while waiting.

Structure is what makes 36 hours work. Clear sprints and roles kept each of us focused, and when it came time to pitch, everyone could speak confidently about their own area.
